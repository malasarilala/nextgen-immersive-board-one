The Education Gap

The Post-SPM Fork in the Road

Every year, hundreds of thousands of young Malaysians sit for SPM. After results, they face a choice — but for B40 youth, it is rarely a free one.

~51%
Continue to diploma, TVET, or degree
Often with financial burden, PTPTN debt, or deferred entry
SPM
~49%
Enter the workforce directly — often without a plan
Driven by financial need, limited guidance, or academic barriers
2019 DOSM data: Out of 560,000 SPM candidates, 390,000 (72.1%) expressed no intention to continue higher education — driven by the need to earn immediately. Parliamentary data from 2021 confirmed 48.74% of that year's SPM batch did not enrol in further education.
Sources: DOSM 2019 SPM Graduate Report; Dewan Rakyat Hansard 2023. Note: the 72.1% measures intent; 48.74% measures confirmed non-enrolment. Most recent available figure: 2022. Verify 2023–2024 data
Three primary reasons for not continuing: immediate income need (family financial pressure), cost of tertiary education, and lack of structured guidance at the post-SPM transition. For B40 families, the decision is rarely about ambition. It is about survival timing.

The Income Reality

The Work Trap

Without qualifications or structured pathways, most B40 youth default to gig work, retail, and F&B. They are earning — but not growing. And what they earn is not enough to live on in urban Malaysia.

The Mismatch

The Skills and Credentials Gap

The Pilot in Action

NextGen Sellers — A Proof of Concept

A structured, employer-linked workforce development programme designed to move B40 youth from the work trap into a sales career — with a career readiness profile they keep for life.

Phase 01
Self-Paced Learning
Sales skills, digital literacy, financial literacy, workplace communication via Kajabi platform
Phase 02
Bootcamp
Intensive facilitated training. DISC profiling. English for Sales. Career Readiness Assessment.
Phase 03
Employer Placement
Paid placement with employer partners. Participants protected under PERKESO.
